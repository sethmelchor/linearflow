# Exposure Simulator — Demo Use Cases

Specific scenarios to walk through during a teaching demonstration, showing how the three sliders (ISO, Aperture, Shutter Speed) combine for different photography genres.

---

## Sports Photography

- **Settings:** ISO 800–1600, f/2.8–4, Shutter 1/1000–1/2000
- **What it teaches:** Fast shutter freezes action, wide aperture lets in enough light to compensate, ISO bumped up to keep exposure balanced
- **Simulator shows:** Well exposed, shallow DOF, no motion blur, slight noise grain appearing

## Landscape Photography

- **Settings:** ISO 100, f/11–16, Shutter 1/30–1/60
- **What it teaches:** Narrow aperture for deep DOF ("everything sharp"), low ISO for clean image, slower shutter is okay because nothing is moving (tripod implied)
- **Simulator shows:** Well exposed, deep/very deep DOF, clean noise-free image

## Portrait Photography

- **Settings:** ISO 100–200, f/1.4–2.8, Shutter 1/125–1/250
- **What it teaches:** Wide aperture creates blurry background (bokeh) to isolate subject, moderate shutter is fine for a still person
- **Simulator shows:** Well exposed, very shallow/shallow DOF, no noise

## Low-Light / Concert Photography

- **Settings:** ISO 3200–6400, f/1.4–2, Shutter 1/60–1/125
- **What it teaches:** Everything cranked to gather light — high ISO introduces visible grain (noise overlay gets heavy), wide aperture, slower shutter. Shows the tradeoff of noise for brightness.
- **Simulator shows:** Noise overlay very visible, shallow DOF, status hovering around well-exposed

## "What Happens When You Get It Wrong" — Overexposure

- **Settings:** ISO 6400, f/1.4, Shutter 1s
- **What it teaches:** All three settings pushing brightness — image blows out completely. Demonstrates that the triangle is a balancing act.
- **Simulator shows:** Overexposed status, extreme brightness, motion blur, heavy noise

## "What Happens When You Get It Wrong" — Underexposure

- **Settings:** ISO 100, f/22, Shutter 1/4000
- **What it teaches:** All three settings restricting light — image goes nearly black
- **Simulator shows:** Underexposed status, very dark image

## The Tradeoff Demo — Same Exposure, Different Look

Walk through keeping the exposure at ~0 EV but shifting *which* slider you move:

1. **ISO 100, f/2.8, 1/125** — shallow DOF, clean
2. **ISO 100, f/8, 1/15** — deep DOF, motion blur appears
3. **ISO 1600, f/8, 1/125** — deep DOF, noise appears

This is the strongest teaching moment — it shows that "well exposed" doesn't mean one right answer. Three different looks, same brightness.
